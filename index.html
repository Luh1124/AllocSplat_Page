Abstract

Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructs renderable scenes from a few posed images, but storing, streaming and rendering them require an output budget chosen by the application. At a smaller budget, each Gaussian must cover more of the scene, with its required extent depending on where the others lie in 3D. We introduce AllocSplat, which allocates capacity through spatial anchors and uses their local spacing to control Gaussian decoding. From the depth and features of a visual geometry model, a non-learned rule selects M anchors among back-projected candidates. Each anchor decodes K Gaussians whose offsets and scales are constrained by its distances to neighbouring selected anchors. Recomputing this local scale and the contextualized decode lets one set of weights serve a range of budgets, emitting exactly KM Gaussians per request. On DL3DV-140, the default budgets achieve higher PSNR and SSIM than the evaluated feed-forward baselines with fewer Gaussians at two, four and six input views. At four views, AllocSplat reaches 23.87 dB with 36.9K Gaussians, 0.99 dB above TokenGS with 85.9% fewer primitives, and 0.29–1.65 dB above ZipSplat at equal counts. Controlled comparisons show that the local scale improves on both fixed-footprint and global budget-scaling controls, and that bounding Gaussian offsets as well as sizes by it helps at every budget.

Method

One checkpoint serves every budget: V = 2, 4 or 6 posed views, M = 256 to 1,944 anchors, K = 36 Gaussians per anchor.

Interactive viewer

Sixteen DL3DV-140 test scenes, reconstructed from two, four or six posed views by one checkpoint without test-time optimization. Notes

One set of weights, several budgets

Each budget recomputes the local scales and the contextualized decode from the same checkpoint and outputs exactly 36M Gaussians. The PSNR shown is the mean over the scene's held-out frames on the common 252×252 evaluation canvas.

Allocation in 3D

Anchors are selected by a non-learned farthest-point rule among the depth points back-projected from the inputs (324 candidates per view). The anchors of a smaller budget are the first M anchors of a larger one; “Replay allocation” adds them in that order.

Spacing sets extent

An anchor's local scale ρ is the mean distance to its 8 nearest selected anchors, clamped to [0.03, 0.5]. Its 36 Gaussian centers are confined to a box of half-width ρ around it, and their scales are capped by ρ. Lower budgets space anchors farther apart, so ρ grows and each group covers more of the scene.
